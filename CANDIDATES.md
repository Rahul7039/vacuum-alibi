# Candidate lines — The Vacuum's Alibi

Judge brief (from the kit): funniest one-line excuse a robot vacuum gives for
missing a spot, weighed on (1) deadpan robot voice, (2) surprise, (3) tightness
of the line, with the punch sitting at the end. Max 200 characters, one line.

Scoring: each candidate rated 1–5 on Deadpan / Surprise / Tightness.

## 1. ★ WINNER
> I did not miss the spot. I classified it as furniture and have been respecting it ever since.
> (93 characters)
- Deadpan 5 — flat denial, then a bureaucratic classification; pure machine voice.
- Surprise 5 — the reframe from "missed dirt" to "furniture" is a turn nobody expects.
- Tightness 5 — every clause earns its place; the punch ("respecting it ever since") lands last.
- Rationale: it is the machine itself talking, smug about its sensors' judgement, and the joke is a specific, unexpected detail rather than a stock dust gag.

## 2.
> I ran a full diagnostic. Conclusion: the spot is load-bearing. Removing it risks structural collapse.
> (101 characters)
- Deadpan 5 — diagnostic-report voice, very robot.
- Surprise 4 — "load-bearing" is a great twist, but the engineering-joke shape is a known pattern.
- Tightness 4 — "Conclusion:" plus two sentences is slightly loose; punch lands well.
- Rationale: strong, but the furniture line's punch is tighter and more surprising.

## 3.
> My cliff sensors identified the spot as a cliff. I do not argue with my cliff sensors.
> (86 characters)
- Deadpan 5 — smug about its sensors, exactly the brief's voice.
- Surprise 4 — cliff sensors misfiring on a flat spot is specific and funny.
- Tightness 4 — the repeat of "cliff sensors" is the joke, but the second sentence is a touch flat.
- Rationale: excellent voice; the punch is a shrug rather than a turn.

## 4.
> The spot filed a restraining order. My sensors are legally obliged to keep a thirty-centimetre distance.
> (104 characters)
- Deadpan 4 — legalistic robot voice works.
- Surprise 4 — a restraining order from a stain is unexpected.
- Tightness 3 — "thirty-centimetre" costs words and the setup is the longest here.
- Rationale: funny premise, but the wording is the baggiest of the shortlist.

## 5.
> I mapped the room forty-seven times. The spot was on none of them. It is either a ghost or a rug.
> (97 characters)
- Deadpan 4 — diligent-mapping robot voice.
- Surprise 3 — "ghost or rug" is a decent turn, but the either/or structure is familiar.
- Tightness 4 — clean three-beat structure, punch at the end.
- Rationale: solid, but the joke lands softer than the furniture classification.

## 6.
> Battery at four percent. I chose to die a hero in the hallway rather than spend my final charge on that crumb.
> (110 characters)
- Deadpan 4 — battery-report opening is very robot.
- Surprise 3 — heroic-sacrifice framing is funny, but "die a hero" is a known gag shape.
- Tightness 3 — longest line; "rather than spend my final charge on that crumb" could be shorter.
- Rationale: good voice, weaker surprise and the loosest line.

## 7.
> The spot and I have an agreement: I pretend not to see it, and it pretends not to be dirt.
> (90 characters)
- Deadpan 3 — reads more like a witty narrator than the machine itself.
- Surprise 3 — a treaty with dirt is a cute observation, not a hard laugh.
- Tightness 5 — perfectly economical, punch at the end.
- Rationale: tight but too cute; the judge wants a joke that lands, not an observation.

## 8.
> I cleaned around it out of respect. It has been there longer than I have, and it outranks me.
> (93 characters)
- Deadpan 4 — "outranks me" is nicely deadpan and smug.
- Surprise 3 — seniority gag; the turn is gentler than the furniture reframe.
- Tightness 4 — clean, punch at the end.
- Rationale: a good line, but the surprise is thinner than the winner's.

## Verdict
Candidate 1 wins: the strongest deadpan voice, the sharpest surprise (dirt
reclassified as furniture), the tightest line, and the punch sits at the very end.
